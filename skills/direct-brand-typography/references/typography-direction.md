# Typography direction

## Serif and sans-serif decisions

- Use a serif display face when the evidence supports editorial, heritage, craft, ceremony, luxury, or expressive fashion positioning.
- Use a sans-serif display face when the evidence supports modernity, utility, sport, technology, clarity, youth, or performance advertising.
- Mix serif and sans-serif only when their roles are clearly different. A reliable pairing is expressive serif headlines with neutral sans-serif supporting copy, price, CTA, and legal text.
- Avoid pairing two families that look almost the same. Create purposeful contrast in construction, width, weight, or texture.
- Do not assume serif means premium or sans-serif means modern; confirm against the actual brand evidence.

## Hierarchy rules

- Use one dominant message, one supporting level, and one action or product-information level.
- Keep headlines to the approved maximum line count; rewrite only from approved alternatives.
- Prefer wrapping and re-layout before shrinking type.
- Use line height that preserves the chosen face's rhythm; display faces can be tighter, while small functional copy needs more air.
- Adjust tracking carefully: large uppercase sans-serif may need modest positive tracking; high-contrast display serifs often need optical rather than mechanical spacing.
- Never fake a logo with a similar font.
- Never substitute a font silently. Record the exact font, fallback, and availability in Figma.

Test hierarchy at 100% export size, in grayscale, and against the busiest intended image area.
